# Mold, Rot & Spoilage Organisms

## Common Mold Types on Fruit
Botrytis (gray mold) commonly affects strawberries and grapes, appearing
as a fuzzy grayish-brown coating. Penicillium mold appears blue-green and
commonly affects citrus fruits. Rhizopus (black bread mold) can affect
stone fruits and appears as black, cottony growth.

## Bacterial Soft Rot
Bacterial soft rot causes fruit tissue to become watery, mushy, and
discolored, often accompanied by a distinctly unpleasant, sometimes
sulfurous, odor. This is common in fruits with any skin puncture or
bruising that allowed bacteria to enter.

## Why Some Rot Is More Dangerous Than Others
Some molds (certain Aspergillus and Penicillium species) can produce
mycotoxins that are not destroyed by cooking and can pose real health
risks even in small quantities. Because a consumer typically cannot
identify which mold species they are looking at, the safest general rule
is: visible mold on soft or high-moisture produce means discard the
entire item.
