# Food Safety Signs in Fresh Produce

## Visual Indicators of Spoilage
Mold growth (fuzzy white, green, blue-green, or black patches), dark
sunken lesions, an unusually wrinkled or shriveled skin combined with soft
mushy flesh, and visible fermentation bubbles are all reliable visual
indicators that a fruit is no longer safe to eat.

## Smell as a Diagnostic Tool
A fermented, alcoholic, or vinegar-like smell almost always indicates the
sugars in the fruit have begun fermenting due to yeast or bacterial
activity. A sharp sour smell paired with visible softening is a strong
signal to discard the item rather than eat it raw.

## Texture Changes
Fresh fruit is typically firm with a slight give when ripe. A fruit that
has become mushy, watery, or leaks juice when lightly pressed has likely
crossed from "ripe" into "spoiling," and any mushy areas should be removed
entirely (or the whole fruit discarded) before considering consumption.

## When Cutting Away Mold Is NOT Enough
For soft, high-moisture fruits (berries, peaches, tomatoes, melons), mold
can send invisible root-like filaments deep into the flesh, so cutting
around a moldy spot does not make the rest of the fruit safe. For firm,
low-moisture produce (hard squash, cabbage), a small isolated mold spot
can sometimes be cut away with a wide margin, but when in doubt, throw it
out.

## Food Safety Disclaimer
This guidance is educational and based on general food-safety best
practices. It is not a substitute for your own senses and judgment, and
individuals with compromised immune systems, who are pregnant, elderly, or
very young should be more conservative and discard borderline produce
rather than risk consuming it.
