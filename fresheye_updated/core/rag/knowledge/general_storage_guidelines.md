# General Fruit & Vegetable Storage Guidelines

## Why Storage Matters
Proper storage slows the natural ripening and decay process by controlling
temperature, humidity, and exposure to ethylene gas. Most produce spoilage
is accelerated by warmth, moisture buildup, and ethylene exposure from
other ripening fruits.

## Refrigeration Rules
Keep berries, leafy greens, broccoli, and cut fruit in the refrigerator
crisper drawer at 1-4°C (34-39°F). Do not refrigerate bananas, tomatoes,
potatoes, onions, or whole melons - cold temperatures damage their texture
and flavor before they even show visible spoilage.

## Ethylene-Producing vs Ethylene-Sensitive Produce
Apples, bananas, avocados, and tomatoes release significant ethylene gas
and ripen other produce faster when stored nearby. Leafy greens, berries,
and herbs are ethylene-sensitive and spoil faster when stored next to
ethylene producers. Store these two groups separately.

## General Signs Something Should Be Discarded
Discard produce immediately if you see fuzzy mold (white, green, or black),
a sour or alcoholic smell, leaking or weeping fluid, or a slimy film on the
surface. These are signs of active microbial growth, not just cosmetic
ripening, and consuming the item carries a real food-safety risk.

## General Signs Something Is Still Safe But Should Be Used Soon
Minor browning, slight softness, small blemishes, or a few dark spots on
an otherwise firm fruit usually mean it is still safe to eat but should be
consumed within 1-2 days, or used in cooked dishes (smoothies, baking,
sauces) where texture changes matter less.
