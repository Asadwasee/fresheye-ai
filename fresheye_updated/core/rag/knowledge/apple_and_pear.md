# Apples & Pears

## Freshness Indicators
A fresh apple or pear has taut, unblemished skin, feels firm and heavy for
its size, and has no soft spots when gently pressed. The stem area should
be intact and not overly shriveled.

## Signs of Spoilage
Brown mushy patches, a wrinkled or leathery skin, an overly sweet
fermented smell, or visible mold around the stem or blossom end all
indicate the fruit has spoiled and should not be eaten raw.

## Storage Advice
Store apples and pears in the refrigerator crisper drawer, ideally in a
perforated bag, away from strong-smelling foods since they can absorb
odors. Apples release ethylene gas, so keep them away from ethylene
sensitive vegetables like leafy greens.

## If Slightly Past Prime
A slightly mealy or soft (but not moldy) apple or pear is generally still
safe to eat and works well cooked - think applesauce, baked apples,
crumbles, or compotes - even if it is no longer pleasant to eat raw.
